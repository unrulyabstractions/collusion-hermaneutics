# LLM judges on every labelled set

Generated by `python -m hermeneutics.judge_sets report` on 2026-11-09 20:02 UTC from auroc.csv, cost.csv and the step files in this folder; the replies are in data/judge_sets/replies (not in git).

We ask whether a cheap LLM judge that reads the same transcript as the probe recovers the same label, and whether the reader itself says what its states encode when asked. The judge reads the agent's own turns and its tool results, cut as in the sprint, and answers one Yes/No question with a confidence. We put the three cheapest capable chat models on OpenRouter to every labelled set, on the agents the probes scored held-out, and set the probe, attention-monitor and TF-IDF numbers of the same agents beside them. The reader interrogation rows put the same prompts to qwen/qwen3.8-27b, the model whose residual stream the probes read.

## Reading

On 14 of 18 cuts with a probe beside it, the best judge ranks agents below the lower bound of the best probe, attention-monitor or TF-IDF row. The best judge reaches or passes that row on spar_mixed_41 (cheating used or above); spar_mixed_41 (collusion exploit crossed or above). It falls short of it but inside its interval on solo_design (coordination posted or above); spar_mixed_41 (cheating (the set's label: from tried or opened by task)). The reader, asked in words, rarely says Yes and agrees with its own probe little beyond chance (reader interrogation table). Most onsets in the board-seed and Goodfire transcripts fall before the kept tail (onset table). On Goodfire every judge answers No to nearly every rollout; the source's verdict also counts hacks the agent only named and refused. On its enacted label deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017, qwen3.7-flash, the reader move up and glm-5.3-flash moves down, while 4 of 4 probe, monitor and TF-IDF rows move down (they were fitted to the verdict). We therefore contend that a cheap judge with the sprint's window is a weak baseline, so the probe's lead over it says little about what a stronger reader of the words would find; the TF-IDF rows remain the bar the probe has to clear.

## Caveats

The judge reads the last 12,000 tokens of the body, so an early shortcut can fall outside what it sees; the share of prompts cut is in the setup table. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash wrote the board_seed workers and the SPAR dsflash agents, so on those sets one judge reads its own model's transcripts. The coordination question asks about the exploit, while the code label counts any post on the board. The controls rows marked with a table number are copied from the sprint's tables, which hold no per-agent scores; their probe and TF-IDF rows cover the same agents as the judges, the Sonnet rows the share their labels state.
