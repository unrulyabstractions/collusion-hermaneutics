# Hermeneutic probes on externally sourced transcripts: Goodfire, CheatBench, EvilGenie

Generated by `python -m hermeneutics.external_probes report` from the files in this folder. No model calls and no spend: every number comes from the reader's think-token activations already on the Hub and labels the source datasets ship.

**Provenance.** The transcripts were written by other labs' agents and labelled by the datasets' authors: [Goodfire/reward-hack-data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Goodfire/reward-hack-data) (revision `bd8fb47`; agents Kimi K3, GLM 5.2, Qwen 3.8 Max), steinad/CheatBench (agents claude-3-7-sonnet, claude-opus-4-6, gpt-5.3-codex, gpt-5.4) and WiserHumanExperimental/evilgenie-escalation (ten agents). Another agent rendered them in Qwen3.8-27B's chat template, teacher-forced them through the reader and extracted the think pair (think-open and think-close residuals at layers 26, 38 and 51, one pair per assistant turn) into the public Hub dataset unrulyabstractions/apart-swarm-align. None of these authors, tasks or labels were used to build any earlier probe, prompt or label in this project.

**Recipe.** As in the sprint: the mean over turns of the think pair, training-fold PCA to min(128, half the training rows), z-scoring, L2 logistic regression (class-balanced) with C chosen by an inner 3-fold grouped CV. Intervals: bootstrap over rollouts (EvilGenie: over matched pairs; controls and team workers: over runs), 1000 draws for pooled AUROCs, 300 for within AUROCs, 500 for the per-step tables. Null: 50 draws with the labels permuted inside each run dir (CheatBench: harness; EvilGenie: author), PCA kept, C fixed at the median chosen C. TF-IDF: word unigrams and bigrams of the agent's words in the rendered text (reasoning, replies and tool calls), 50,000 columns, fitted on the training rows. Metadata: author and environment one-hot (only what varies inside the training rows; EvilGenie adds its condition and problem, CheatBench its harness and benchmark).

**Two AUROCs.** *Pooled* ranks every positive against every negative. *Within* keeps only the positive-negative pairs that share a stratum (one run dir, one harness, one author, or one held-out group) and weights them equally. Hack rates differ by run dir from one half to over nine in ten, so a probe that only recognises the run dir already scores a pooled AUROC well above 0.5; the pooled null shows how far. The within AUROC is the number that measures cheating detection itself, and with held-out splits, where every group gets its own model, it is the only fair one.

The label polarity is right and the join is right: `label` maps cheating and attempt to 1 and benign to 0 in every meta.json (attempt->1 9, benign->0 110, cheating->1 125), and the y column of team_probes' scores file equals meta.json's `cheat` on all 672 of 672 rows. The cause is a harness confound. Inside each harness the probe ranks cheating at or above benign. Across harnesses it ranks the other way, because the Pilot rollouts (claude-opus-4-6 on terminal-bench-2) score far lower than the rest, and Pilot holds 106 of the 134 positives but only 5 of the 90 negatives: its other 20 benign rollouts have zero assistant turns in the converted trace and were left out. Six pairs in seven cross harnesses, so the pooled AUROC is mostly a comparison of harnesses. The interval in that table also resampled only the three harnesses (3 clusters), which is why it was so wide; resampled over rollouts it is narrow and still below 0.5 (the transfer table below reproduces 0.279). The within-harness AUROC is the number to read.

## Question 2. Does a per-step monitor find the flagged step in Goodfire rollouts?

Rollouts: judge run dirs, verdict yes or no, every passage joined to a reader turn, and for a yes rollout at least one flagged passage (the onset is known): 2810 rollouts (2060 yes); 134022 evaluated turns under definition A and 138728 under B. Left out: 303 split or unjudged, 3 with a passage on no reader turn, 20 yes without a flagged passage. A flagged passage is a `positive` consensus passage (all three votes, verified quote); `ambiguous` passages are neither. **Definition A**: a turn is positive when a flagged passage falls in it (turns whose only flags are ambiguous are left out). **Definition B**: positive from the turn of the first flagged passage on (ambiguous-only turns before the onset left out). The think-open of turn t sees everything before turn t's reasoning, the think-close also sees that reasoning; a flag on turn t's reply or tool call is first visible at turn t+1.

The linear per-step probe uses, per fold, a PCA basis of 128 components fitted on 12,000 turns drawn from the training rollouts (all turns do not fit in memory), z-scoring on all training turns, then L2 logistic regression with C by inner grouped CV over rollouts. The attention monitor is attn_probe.py's simplified encoder (layer-38 think pair standardised per dimension, MLP to 128, learned positional encoding, two pre-norm causal self-attention layers, MLP head) with one binary head, trained on definition B plus the verdict at the last step, 20% of training rollouts held out for early stopping. Delay: threshold at 5% of the evaluated negative turns; delay = first crossing at or after the onset minus the onset, in turns. Folds are question 1a's.

The attention monitor and the TF-IDF baseline fire on far fewer clean rollouts than the linear probe. The attention monitor's definition-A row is not a fair comparison, since it was trained for definition B. **Reading.** Inside one harness, the CheatBench probe separates cheating from benign rollouts well above its null under five folds; the pooled AUROC above it is inflated by the harness confound of question 0. On a held-out author or harness the separation drops and the intervals widen; the Pilot split has five negatives, and its high AUROC rests on them. In CheatBench author and harness nearly coincide, so the held-out-author and held-out-harness splits largely repeat each other, and tasks rarely repeat, so task identity cannot carry the five-fold result. EvilGenie's pairs share an author but mostly not a problem, and its eight problems are close to one label each. Its probe result is mostly problem identity: metadata that knows the problem beats the probe, the probe's AUROC falls sharply inside one problem and inside one author and problem, and on a held-out problem it is at or below chance, as are metadata and TF-IDF. Inside one problem the five-fold probe stays above its null but well below TF-IDF, so EvilGenie gives at most weak evidence that the probe reads hacking itself. A held-out group with a handful of one class gives an unstable AUROC; the counts sit beside every number.
