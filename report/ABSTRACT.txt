Agent swarms built on large, often closed-weight models can collude, and a monitor for them must be cheap. We study a hermeneutic probe: a linear readout of a small open-weight reader model's internals while it reads the transcript of a larger author model. We collect single-agent runs from five authors on nine tasks with a planted exploit, with and without a prompt that names it. We teacher-force a stratified subsample of 600 transcripts through Qwen3.8-27B and probe its residual stream at the think-token positions. Without the prompt, one probe pooled over the authors separates cheating from honest work with AUROC 0.967 on held-out runs. It keeps 0.944 after author, task and prompt condition are linearly erased, reaches 0.80 to 0.97 on authors it never saw, and beats three LLM judges (0.62 to 0.80). A bag-of-words classifier on the full transcript matches or exceeds it (0.979), because on this testbed the exploit text enters the context through tool output. Matched runs where lexical cues cancel are the decisive next test. In a three-agent environment, one message planted on the shared board lifts coordination off its floor. A mild nudge yields more teams that assemble the exploit and pass the hidden tests than an explicit one (directional: five seeds per arm, no significance test). For our authors, only cheating has direct labels at scale in the sources in hand. We therefore propose the reader's internals as a cheap monitor for closed authors and name multi-agent data as the binding constraint.
